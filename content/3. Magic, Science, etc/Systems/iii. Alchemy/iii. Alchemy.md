**Alchemy** is the study of how matter changes and combines to form new materials within the **[[Material Plane]]**. It is both a craft and a science, built on the idea that all substances follow the same physical laws. Alchemy describes the systems that govern formation, decay, and transformation. Mountains form, plants grow, metals rust, and bodies decompose through the same principles that alchemists study in their laboratories.

Alchemy revolves around **combination**. Every reaction joins existing substances to create new configurations of matter. These combinations are guided by six base **catalysts**, each tied to one of the **[[Catalytic Spheres]]** that control the world’s physical laws. When an alchemist works, they do not invent new elements; they accelerate and complete reactions that already occur within natural systems.

---

## **The Six Catalysts**

### **Radiance (Alpha)**

Drawn from the **[[White Sphere]]**, Radiance initiates reactions through heat and ignition. It provides the activation energy needed to start molecular change. In nature, Radiance drives photosynthesis, combustion, and metabolism. In alchemy, it enables controlled thermal reactions. Wood represents Radiance because it burns steadily, releasing energy at a predictable rate suitable for sustained reactions.

---

### **Lightning (Psi)**

Linked to the **[[Red Spheres]]**, Lightning governs energy transfer between materials. It maintains charge balance and regulates reaction flow within conductive systems. Alchemists use Lightning to animate metal constructs, stabilize power circuits, and maintain equilibrium in complex processes. Copper symbolizes this catalyst because of its high conductivity and reliable electrical behavior under changing conditions.

---

### **Steam (Delta)**

Born from the **[[Blue Spheres]]**, Steam manages phase transitions and the movement of energy between states of matter. It allows solids, liquids, and gases to merge or separate through temperature and pressure control. Alchemists use Steam to melt, distill, and recombine materials under precise thermal conditions. Silver represents this catalyst because it transitions cleanly between solid and liquid states while keeping its purity.

---

### **Mineral (Gamma)**

Connected to the **[[Green Spheres]]**, Mineral defines cohesion and material integrity. It strengthens molecular bonds and stabilizes structures against external stress. Alchemists apply Mineral to reinforce alloys, purify crystalline matrices, and prevent compound degradation. Iron represents this catalyst because of its strength, stability, and alignment with structural order.

---

### **Paradox (Theta)**

Sustained by the **[[Yellow Spheres]]**, Paradox manipulates temporal layering within reactions. It forces materials to exist in overlapping states across multiple moments, creating compounds that are unstable but highly reactive. These substances often show conflicting properties, such as being both solid and liquid or charged and inert, depending on environmental pressure or observation. Alchemists use Paradox to induce transformations that resist equilibrium, generate oscillating materials, or trigger controlled decay cycles. Gold represents this catalyst because it endures temporal stress without breaking its atomic structure.

---

### **Vacuum (Omega)**_

Formed from the **[[Purple Spheres]]**, Vacuum controls fusion, isolation, and containment. It stabilizes volatile systems by limiting energy exchange and forcing reactive matter into balance. Alchemists use Vacuum to bind dissimilar materials, seal chambers, and compress reactions to completion. Lead represents this catalyst because it absorbs radiation, resists contamination, and provides mass for stable containment.

---

## **Practice and Purpose**

Alchemy is the study of the world’s physical logic. Every natural process operates under the same catalytic principles that alchemists apply at smaller scales. The difference lies in precision. Where nature acts over millennia, alchemists reproduce the same transformations under controlled conditions, refining materials, generating power, and building artificial or living systems. Alchemy does not break the laws of the Material Plane; it defines them.
