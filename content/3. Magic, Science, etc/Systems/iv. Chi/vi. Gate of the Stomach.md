**Gate of the Stomach** is the sixth of the Six Gates of Chi, aligned with the **[[Purple Spheres]]** and the principle of transformation. It governs conversion, endurance, and the ability to turn burden into strength. Through this gate, practitioners learn to absorb and repurpose all forms of energy, transforming harm, hunger, or pain into fuel for balance and survival.

The stomach represents the meeting of intake and renewal. When the Gate of the Stomach is opened, chi circulates through cycles of consumption and change, dissolving what is harmful and refining what is useful. This process mirrors the power of the Purple Spheres, which nullify excess and restore equilibrium through absorption. Practitioners who master this gate can draw vitality from exhaustion, resist corruption, or reclaim stability after spiritual collapse.

In practice, the gate is opened through deep breathing, grounding posture, and the acceptance of discomfort without resistance. By focusing on the center of gravity within the body, practitioners learn to contain and redirect tension instead of rejecting it. As the gate strengthens, chi becomes dense and self-sustaining, allowing the spirit to endure long periods without food, rest, or external support.

The Gate of the Stomach is the seat of resilience. When closed, energy disperses, leaving the body weak and unfocused. When open, chi gathers into a steady core that supports all other gates. It is through this center that the practitioner learns patience, adaptation, and the quiet power of endurance.

In nature, the gate’s influence can be seen in compost turning decay into fertile soil, in volcanic stone forming from molten earth, and in creatures that thrive by consuming what others cannot. To those who study chi, it is the gate of endurance and transformation, the final threshold before the **[[Monastic Paths]]**, where energy no longer fades but renews itself.
