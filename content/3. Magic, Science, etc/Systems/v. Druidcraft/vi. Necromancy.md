**Necromancy** is the study of death and stillness through the **[[Purple Spheres]]** and the central **nul sphere**, which mark the boundary between motion and stasis. It is the final discipline of **[[Druidcraft]]**, concerned with the balance between life and what remains after it. Necromancy does not seek to conquer death but to understand its place in the natural cycle, treating it as a necessary state rather than an ending.

The Purple Spheres stabilize decay, rest, and memory across the **[[Mortal Plane]]**, ensuring that what dies returns to stillness instead of chaos. Druids who follow this path learn to listen to what persists beyond the body, communicating with the traces left behind. They act as guides for souls that linger near the **[[Astral Plane]]**, helping them move toward their proper resting place.

Necromancers describe death as the pause between breaths, a moment where all energy gathers before returning to its origin. Through the nul sphere, they can sense this transition, tracing where the spirit detaches and where the soul continues. Some use this awareness to purify corrupted land or still restless dead, while others preserve memory by drawing the echoes of the departed into living matter.

The practice requires restraint and precision. Necromancy is not about creating life or binding spirits but maintaining the threshold that separates one state from another. Those who overstep risk breaking that boundary, allowing what should rest to move again without purpose.

To study Necromancy is to understand that stillness is as vital as growth. Every flame fades, every tide falls, and every life returns to silence. The druid’s task is to keep that silence unbroken and to ensure the world continues its rhythm between living and dying.
