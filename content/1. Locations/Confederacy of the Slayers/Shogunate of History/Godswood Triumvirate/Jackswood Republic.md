**Jackswood Republic** is a sprawling urban expanse located in the southern basin of the **[[Godswood Triumvirate]]**, near the waters separating the **[[Shogunate of History]]** from the **[[Gloam]]**. The city is built on uneven, waterlogged ground where mud, roots, and debris have layered for centuries. Entire districts rest on the ruins of older ones, supported by tangled root systems and collapsed foundations. From a distance, the skyline appears tiered, with broken spires and bridges rising above the mist like pieces of several cities stacked together.

The ground beneath Jackswood is unstable. Pools of resin and water gather between cracked stone and rotted wood, while copper vines from the **[[Jackwood Trees]]** wind through the structures, reinforcing them in some places and pulling them apart in others. The trees grow everywhere, through walls, rooftops, and stairwells, each bearing copper bulbs that glow faintly and drop without warning, leaving burn marks and metallic dust across the streets.

Under the surface, an older city lies sealed in amber. Entire buildings and streets are preserved within the resin layers, visible through the translucent walls of the lower tunnels. Many of these capsules contain human figures, frozen in motion or sealed in ritual positions. Excavations into these layers are common but dangerous, as cutting into the resin releases toxic fumes and destabilizes the structures above.

The current city extends upward in makeshift layers of wood, amber, and metal. Walkways, rope bridges, and fungal platforms connect the higher levels, while the lower streets sink gradually into the mud. The air smells of copper and decay, and a dull orange light filters through the resin that covers the older ruins. No central plan or authority governs the layout; new districts are built wherever there is space to stand, and old ones are left to collapse or sink.

The **Jackswood Republic** functions as a continuous cycle of construction and decay. New homes rise on the remnants of the old, and the forest’s roots grow through every layer. The result is a living vertical maze where the line between architecture and terrain has long been lost.
