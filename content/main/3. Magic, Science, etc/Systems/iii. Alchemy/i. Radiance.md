**Radiance** is the first and most direct of the six alchemical catalysts, drawn from the **[[White Sphere]]**. It represents ignition, growth, and the release of stored energy. Radiance begins reactions by heating or burning reagents until they awaken, allowing matter to change its state and reveal its potential. It is not limited to fire alone but describes the spark that sets any process in motion, whether natural or artificial.

In living systems, Radiance fuels both growth and decay. Plants use it to convert light into matter, drawing strength from sunlight through processes that mirror alchemical ignition. Animals rely on it to break down food, releasing stored energy to sustain movement and thought. To the alchemist, this same principle applies to every reaction: all transformation begins with the spark of Radiance.

Wood is the material most closely tied to this catalyst. It burns evenly, stores heat, and serves as a reliable foundation for ignition-based reactions. Many alchemical furnaces, arrays, and early-stage experiments rely on wooden constructs to channel Radiance safely. In laboratory settings, Radiance is used to smelt metals, purify compounds, and initiate other catalytic processes that require activation energy.

Among all catalysts, Radiance is the most fundamental. It begins the cycle of reaction, setting other catalysts in motion. Without it, no process, living or artificial, can start.
