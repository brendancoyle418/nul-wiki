**Gate of the Lungs** is the third of the Six Gates of Chi, aligned with the **[[Blue Spheres]]** and the principle of exchange. It governs the balance between stillness and motion, teaching how to draw in and release chi through breath and awareness. When opened, this gate connects the practitioner’s inner flow to the wider currents of the **[[Ethereal Plane]]**, allowing them to maintain equilibrium between what is taken in and what is released.

The lungs represent transition and renewal. Each breath marks the exchange between fullness and emptiness, life and rest. When the Gate of the Lungs is open, chi moves through the body like air across water, flowing and settling in rhythm. This cycle sustains every other gate by keeping energy in motion and preventing stagnation.

In practice, the gate is opened through slow, measured breathing that follows natural rhythm. Practitioners learn to feel the weight and motion of their breath, sensing how chi gathers on inhalation and disperses on exhalation. As mastery deepens, breath becomes a direct vessel for energy, able to purge corruption, calm emotion, or draw strength from the surrounding flow of life.

The Gate of the Lungs is also the center of endurance and adaptation. It teaches how to recover balance after strain and how to let tension pass through without harm. Without it, chi becomes trapped, leading to fatigue or collapse. With it, energy circulates freely, restoring vitality and steadiness.

In nature, this gate is reflected in wind through open plains, tides that rise and fall, and the drifting of clouds before rain. To those who follow the discipline of chi, it is the gate of renewal and release, the reminder that balance is maintained through constant motion and return.
