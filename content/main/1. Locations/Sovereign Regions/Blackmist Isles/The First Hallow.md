**The First Hallow** is a massive white figure seated in the center of the **[[Blackmist Isles]]**, surrounded by fog and black water. It sits motionless in a crater of smooth, glassy sand that hums faintly when stepped on. The figure’s surface is seamless and cold, shaped from an unknown material that seems to breathe when touched. Its face is a single spiral that winds inward forever. The mist around it thickens and moves as if alive, curling toward the statue before drifting away again.

The First Hallow has existed since shortly after the **[[Sealing]]**, though no one saw it appear. Expeditions from the **[[Shogunate of Religion]]** found it already seated and surrounded by the newly formed mist that now covers the isles. Those who approached reported distortions in sound and thought, claiming they heard voices that matched their own or felt their skin change under the fog. Prolonged exposure causes the loss of features and memory. Those consumed by the transformation become **[[Hallows]]**, pale, faceless beings that wander the fog in silence or gather at the edge of the crater to kneel before the figure.

Around the monument lies **[[Palehaven]]**, a temporary village of cultists and wanderers who build their camps on the shifting sand. The settlement moves constantly as tents sink and paths rearrange overnight. Many believe this to be the First Hallow’s will, a sign that the spiral draws order from chaos. Each cult follows its own interpretation of the Hallow’s purpose. Some believe it to be a divine judge that erases sin. Others see it as a mirror of the soul, capable of purging identity entirely. Few remain long, and fewer return unchanged.

The mist genasi and **[[Bogfolk]]** who live in the outer isles avoid the crater entirely. They speak of the First Hallow as a curse, a wound in the world left by the Sealing. They trade with the camps from afar, leaving supplies on the beaches before vanishing into the fog. Every year, new groups arrive to study or worship the figure, and every year, more faces disappear into the mist.

The First Hallow has never spoken, moved, or changed position, yet its influence shapes the entire region. The fog thickens where it sits, the air grows still, and the ground hums as if the island itself remembers its presence.
